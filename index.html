Motivation

Can we teach a VLA model 3D geometry while preserving its representation capacity?
Direct feature alignment can collapse the VLA feature space.
RIGID aligns spatial relationships to transfer geometry while preserving expressivity.

Abstract

Spatial understanding is essential for robotic manipulation. Existing methods transfer 3D knowledge to vision-language-action (VLA) models by directly aligning their features with a pretrained 3D foundation model. We observe that this alignment can act as low-rank regularization, reducing the VLA’s representation capacity. We introduce RIGID (Rotation-Invariant Geometry Distillation), which aligns pairwise spatial relationships through single-view and cross-view attention. An optimal transport objective transfers geometric structure while allowing the VLA to retain its own feature basis. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, our LIBERO-Probing benchmark, and real-world manipulation show improved spatial representations and robustness while preserving representation capacity. RIGID uses RGB observations at inference, without additional depth sensors.

Method Overview

Distill the geometry in how image patches relate to one another.

Single-view attention captures local spatial structure; cross-view attention captures correspondences across cameras. A geometry-aware optimal transport loss compares these attention distributions using distances between image patches. The underlying relational objective is invariant to orthogonal transformations in feature space, avoiding a fixed teacher feature basis.

Real-world Experiments

Six kitchen manipulation tasks on a Franka Emika Panda, using wrist and side-view RGB cameras. With a π₀ backbone, RIGID achieves the highest reported average success rate among the three evaluated methods.
